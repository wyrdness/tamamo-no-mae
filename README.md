## Overview

Tamamo-no-Mae is a legendary figure of late-medieval Japanese narrative, first appearing in Muromachi-period otogizōshi and elaborated through the Edo period. In the canonical legend she is a nine-tailed fox (kyūbi no kitsune) who, having previously corrupted Chinese and Indian rulers in earlier lives (often identified with the Shang concubine Daji and with 'Lady Kayō' of India), arrives in Japan and serves Emperor Konoe (or, in many later sources, Emperor Toba) as the courtesan Tamamo-no-Mae. She is so beautiful and erudite that the Emperor falls under her spell and falls gravely ill. The astrologer Abe no Yasuchika divines her true nature, and the warriors Kazusa-no-suke (Hirotsune) and Miura-no-suke (Yoshiaki) are dispatched to hunt her on the Nasu plain in Shimotsuke (modern Tochigi prefecture), where she is killed. Her vengeful spirit then enters a stone, the Sesshō-seki ('Killing Stone'), which emits poisonous vapours and kills any creature that approaches. The stone is finally exorcised by the Buddhist monk Gennō Shinshō, who shatters it into fragments. The Sesshō-seki at Nasu split visibly in two on March 5, 2022, an event widely reported in Japanese and international press. Some scholarship connects Tamamo-no-Mae to the historical Empress consort Bifukumon-in (Fujiwara no Nariko, 1117–1160).

## Notable Sightings

### Splitting of the Sesshō-seki ⭐
> **Date**: 2022-03-05  
> **Location**: Sesshō-seki ('Killing Stone'), Nasu Yumoto Onsen, Nasu town, Tochigi prefecture  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (Park visitors, Tochigi prefectural cultural-property staff)  
> **Evidence**: Photographs; Tochigi prefectural reports  
The Sesshō-seki ('Killing Stone'), the volcanic rock at Nasu Yumoto traditionally identified with the death-place and lingering spirit of Tamamo-no-Mae, was discovered to have visibly split into two pieces on the morning of March 5, 2022. Tochigi prefectural officials attributed the rupture to long-term weathering and water infiltration; commentators noted the cultural resonance with the legend of the fox-spirit's release.
**Credibility**: high — A real geological event with a strong folkloric resonance.
**Sources**: Asahi Shimbun (March 2022); BBC News (March 2022)

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Bifukumon-in identification** _(Cited in the English-language Wikipedia article on Tamamo-no-Mae)_

Some commentators have proposed that the Tamamo-no-Mae figure is a literary refraction of the historical empress consort Fujiwara no Nariko (Bifukumon-in, 1117–1160), whose political power around Emperor Toba and Emperor Konoe drew controversy.

**Volcanic gas explanation for Sesshō-seki** _(Japanese volcanological surveys)_

The Nasu Yumoto area is volcanically active and genuinely emits hydrogen sulfide and other gases lethal to small animals, providing a naturalistic origin for the 'killing stone' tradition.
